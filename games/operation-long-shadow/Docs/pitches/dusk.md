# OPERATION LONG SHADOW — Mission Pitch (`dusk`)

> **Logline:** October 1984. A four-person black-ops team pushes through a sun-baked market town at golden hour to rip a numbers station's codebook out of a warlord's fortified telegraph compound. The sun goes down during the raid. The station starts broadcasting at sunset, and it's reading numbers on the team's own radio net. Strapped in a chair months later, Sgt. Nathan Calder keeps telling his interrogator the book was on the helicopter. The interrogator already knows where the book is. He wants to know who else read it.

Angle: **Dusk town assault and intel recovery.** Golden hour turns to blue hour while you fight, three pieces of intel sit in one compound (ledgers, film reels, codebook), and the mission ends with a scripted helicopter exfil under fire.

## 1. Setting

### The Republic of Sardahn (fictional)
Sardahn is a landlocked desert republic of salt pans, basalt ridges and old caravan roads. It gained independence in the 1950s and its government has been coup-of-the-month ever since. In 1984 the capital controls the cities and very little else. Out on the border, whoever holds the customs posts holds the money. Sardahn has its own (invented) language, **Sardani**, and its own invented script. **All signage, posters and graffiti use the invented script or Latin letters, never a real alphabet.** That way no real text, religious or otherwise, ends up on a wall by accident.

### Zaroud
Zaroud is a town of about 4,000 people on a low shelf above a dry salt pan. It grew up around three things:
- **The Weighbridge Gate**, a customs arch with a truck scale where everything crossing the border once got weighed and taxed.
- **The Long Market**, a north–south covered market street that ends at **Clock Square**. The colonial-era clock tower there stopped at **6:40** years ago and nobody fixed it. The square also has the **Cinema Zenith**, a faded 1950s picture house with a marquee.
- **The Zaroud Relay Station**, a 1930s Art Deco telegraph and cable relay station with a **90 m lattice radio mast**. It was built by a defunct private company, the *Trans-Desert Telegraph & Cable Co.*, whose name is still painted on the façade in faded letters. Today it's the headquarters of Colonel Harrou, and it hosts a numbers station Western intelligence calls **"the Clockmaker"**.

**Why the streets are empty:** Harrou declared a curfew at 17:30. The siren is winding down when the mission starts, and the townspeople are behind locked shutters. The town reads as **lived-in and occupied, not hostile**: laundry on the lines, kids' chalk drawings, cinema posters for invented films, radios murmuring behind shutters, food cooling on a café table. The player never fights in a home and never sees a civilian harmed.

**Sensitivity guardrails (design rules):**
- No religious buildings, imagery or audio. The skyline landmarks are a clock tower, a radio mast, a water tower, the cinema marquee and a grain silo. No domes or towers that read as places of worship. No call to prayer.
- No real flags, insignia, militant groups or national armies. Enemies are a mercenary private army plus unmarked foreign advisors. Real-world weapons appear (AKM, RPG-7) because that's the period, but nobody wears a national identity.
- Locals are victims of Harrou, not enemies. Sparrow speaks Sardani and treats the town with respect.
- No keffiyeh-as-villain shorthand. The Legion's silhouette comes from mismatched surplus camo, berets and boonie hats, aviator goggles and **orange customs armbands**.

## 4. Premise and interrogation framing

**Now (the chair):** It's 22 October 1984, 03:12, somewhere underground. Calder is strapped into a steel chair with electrodes taped to his forearms and six mismatched CRT monitors in front of him. A wall clock behind them is stopped at **6:40**. The Interrogator stays behind the player, unseen: a voice, cigarette smoke, a pen tapping in groups of four. He only wants to know one thing: *where is the codebook?*

**Then (the mission):** TALLOW briefed SUNDIAL to recover three items from the Zaroud Relay Station. Harrou captured the Clockmaker numbers station and was about to sell its codebook to the Greycoats. The team was told the book lists a foreign network's agents, and that the station starts its nightly broadcast at sunset, **18:40**. "You will be gone before the Clockmaker opens his mouth."

**The truth (revealed over the mission):**
1. The **Ledgers** (Beat 7) show Harrou's arms shipments were countersigned by "P." That's Penhallow, TALLOW himself. Hollis recognizes the signature.
2. The **S-3 Reels** (Beat 8) are 16mm film of *Calder himself*, months earlier, strapped in this same chair under this same stopped clock: "Subject S-3, Session 11."
3. The **Codebook** (Beat 9) ends with a roster of LONG SHADOW sleepers, each with a four-digit activation group. **SUNDIAL-3 / CALDER, N. / 0640.**
4. The broadcast on the squad net at sunset, and again during exfil, isn't coming from Zaroud. After Calder destroys the Zaroud transmitter, the numbers keep coming: TALLOW is activating his asset.
5. On the helicopter, the activated Calder raises his pistol at Hollis, the one man who knew the signature. We cut to white before the shot. In the chair, Calder says "RPG." The Interrogator says, "The crew says the RPG missed."

**How the framing intrudes during play** (this is the "glitch insert" system: 0.4–1.5 s cuts back to the chair, scanline tear, an audio sting, gameplay frozen):

| When | What the player sees | Line |
|---|---|---|
| After the silent pump-house shots (B2) | Interrogator's pen on the steel table, mid-tap | #14 "Clean. Quiet…" |
| Sunset numbers on the radio (B5) | The stopped clock at 6:40, a 6-frame flash synced to "Six. Four—" | #36 |
| Film reel (B8) | The projected room *becomes* the real room for 3 s | #46 |
| Helicopter (B11) | A rapid cascade of 4 inserts while the pistol rises | (cut to white) |

**The ending turn:** The Interrogator reads 0-6-4-0. Calder's fists unclench, and he calmly gives up Sparrow ("She photographed every page."). Then the Interrogator picks up a phone and says "Magpie, Tallow. Prep the aircraft. One more pickup." That's the moment the voice clicks for the player. The debrief dossier lists **CALDER, N. — ASSET STATUS: ACTIVE**.

**Player tuning (CoD arcade):** Health regenerates after 4 s out of fire. The player goes down in about 5 rifle hits on Regular. Enemies die to 2–4 body hits or 1 headshot. Sprint, slide (crouch while sprinting), auto-mantle at chest height, ADS, and frags cooked by holding G. **Squadmates are invulnerable.** Their kill share is tuned to about 25% so the player does most of the killing. They follow designer-placed **node chains**: each beat has cover-node sets A/B/C that advance on triggers or when an area is cleared. If they fall more than 25 m behind while off-screen, they teleport to catch up.

### Beat 1 — THE SHOULDER (ridge overlook)
- **Location:** Rocky basalt shoulder 22 m above town at the map's SW corner. A scree wadi runs down NE to the grove.
- **Light:** 17:58, sun at 8° elevation and 258° azimuth, so it's **behind the player**. Zaroud below glows ochre and every building throws a long shadow NE toward the mast. Dust haze softens everything past 250 m.
- **Gameplay:** The player starts prone, the title card plays, then they kneel. **Binocular briefing:** hold RMB to glass three points of interest (POIs). Each one gets marked on the HUD as it's observed, so the briefing turns into the mission's markers: (1) the relay station and mast, (2) the clock tower, (3) the Weighbridge Gate. Then the player follows Hollis down the wadi. The route teaches **slide** (a scree chute prompts "Sprint + C") and **mantle** (a fallen boulder).
- **Squad:** Hollis on point with a FOLLOW marker. Sparrow photographs the town. Tinker extends the PRC-77 whip antenna and coils it back up.
- **Encounter:** None. The curfew siren winds down. Harrou's PA echoes across the town.
- **Objectives / markers:** `OBSERVE ZAROUD 0/3` → `FOLLOW HOLLISTER` → `REACH THE GROVE` (diamond marker, 140 m).
- **Radio:** #07–#11.
- **Transition:** The wadi bottoms out in a concrete irrigation ditch. A crouch prompt fires as the grove's edge comes into view.

### Beat 2 — PUMP HOUSE SIX (grove and Weighbridge Gate)
- **Location:** A 75 x 60 m pomegranate and olive grove with irrigation channels and a diesel pump house. On the far side is the Weighbridge Gate: a stone arch, a truck scale plate, a barrier boom, a static Legion cargo truck with a PKM on its bed, and a guard hut with a cassette radio playing.
- **Light:** 18:08, 6°. Sun shafts slant through the tree canopy (fake shaft cards plus dust motes). Orange armbands flare in the light.
- **Gameplay:** **Silent opener.** Hollis tags two sentries smoking at the pump house. The player draws the Mk 22 Hush Puppy and they fire together on "Mark." If the player misses or fires early, the sentries alert the patrol, no fail state. Then the team moves through the grove to the gate, where a 3-man patrol and the truck gunner hold the arch. It's a loud, short fight that teaches cover, ADS, regen and the frag throw.
- **Squad:** Hollis takes the right sentry in sync. In the gate fight, Sparrow and Tinker suppress from the irrigation wall (node A), then advance to the guard hut (node B) once the gunner is dead.
- **Encounter (6):** 2 Legion sentries. At the gate: 3 Legion Riflemen and 1 Legion Gunner on the truck. Peak alive 4.
- **Objectives / markers:** `ELIMINATE THE SENTRIES` (2 red X markers, tagged by Hollis) → `CLEAR THE WEIGHBRIDGE` → `ENTER ZAROUD`.
- **Radio:** #12–#17. Glitch insert #14 plays after the silent kills.
- **Transition:** Tinker bolt-cuts the chained gate with a short squad animation. The gate creaks open on the Long Market: long shadow bands across the street, hanging lanterns, dust in the light.

### Beat 3 — THE LONG MARKET
- **Location:** An 80 m north–south market street, 6–8 m wide, with 2–3 storey plastered houses on both sides. Arcades, wooden balconies, fabric awnings, stalls of oranges and pomegranates, brass coffee pots, rolled rugs, cables strung across the street, a café with chairs left mid-meal. Halfway along, the **Crossing** is an open gap with no awnings. **Tinsmiths' Lane** is a 4 m wide side alley on the west.
- **Light:** 18:17, 4.5°. The sun comes from the west, so the **west façades cast the street into shadow while the east façades burn gold.** Every cross-alley drops a bright band of sunlight across the street. Combat in the market alternates between deep shade and blinding light.
- **Gameplay:** A pushing gunfight up the street, fought on two levels: the street and the balconies. Stalls are cover. Oranges spill and roll when a crate is shot (cheap physics). Halfway up, the team crosses the open Crossing under white smoke. A half-collapsed awning forces a mantle over a stall.
- **Squad:** The team leapfrogs from arcade to arcade on the left and right. Sparrow calls out the balcony. Tinker throws smoke on #22. Hollis suppresses from the café.
- **Encounter (9):** Wave A has 4 Legion Riflemen behind stalls. The east balcony has 1 Legion Gunner (PKM) and 1 rooftop rifleman. **Wave B** is 3 Legion Riflemen flanking through Tinsmiths' Lane. It triggers once the player passes the Crossing or Wave A is down to 2 alive. Peak alive 6.
- **Objectives / markers:** `PUSH THROUGH THE LONG MARKET` (diamond on the square's arch, distance counts down).
- **Radio:** #18–#25. Harrou's English PA line (#23) booms from horn speakers overhead.
- **Transition:** The street ends at a stone arch. Hollis holds the team at its edge, and the arch frames the clock tower stopped at 6:40.

### Beat 4 — CLOCK SQUARE / CINEMA ZENITH
- **Location:** A 70 x 50 m square with colonial arcades, a dry fountain, and the 22 m stone clock tower (non-religious, four clock faces all at 6:40). A Legion **technical** (a static pickup with a DShK) is parked in the NE corner. On the north side is the **Cinema Zenith**: a box-office lobby with terrazzo floors and posters, a dark auditorium with dust shafts falling through shrapnel holes in the roof, a balcony, and a **marquee walkway** overlooking the square.
- **Light:** 18:26, 3°. The square is mostly in shadow now. Only the tower top and the marquee letters are still lit. While the player is inside the cinema, **the skybox crossfades from golden hour to sunset** (§7).
- **Gameplay:** The DShK pins the squad at the arch. Hollis sends the player alone through a side alley to the cinema's side door. Inside: a 2-enemy lobby ambush, then the auditorium in near-darkness lit by dust shafts and the beam of one flickering EXIT sign, then the balcony stairs. The player comes out on the marquee *above and behind* the gunner and kills him or drops a frag into the truck bed (the truck burns as a static fire prop). Two Greycoats then arrive from the east street. It's the first time the player meets the better enemy.
- **Squad:** Pinned at the arch (node A) firing blind until the gunner dies, then they flow into the square (node B) and fight the Greycoats with the player.
- **Encounter (8):** 1 Technical Gunner, 3 Legion Riflemen in the square, 2 Legion Riflemen in the lobby, then 2 Greycoat Advisors. Peak alive 6.
- **Objectives / markers:** `FLANK THROUGH THE CINEMA` (marker on the side door) → `NEUTRALIZE THE TECHNICAL` (red diamond on the gunner, visible through walls) → `REGROUP WITH HOLLISTER`.
- **Radio:** #26–#29.
- **Transition:** Hollis points the team to a steel ladder up the cinema's flank: "Up top."

### Beat 5 — LAST LIGHT (the Laundry Roofs)
- **Location:** A 55 x 30 m run of flat roofs between 6 and 10 m high. Low parapets, water tanks, TV antennas, laundry lines with sheets lifting in the wind, a pigeon loft. Gaps between roofs are 2.5 m or less (sprint-jump with auto-mantle). It ends at a roof overlooking the station's west wall.
- **Light:** 18:34 → 18:40. The sun drops from 1.5° to 0°. **This is the signature moment** (cinematic MID-A, §6.3).
- **Gameplay:** Rooftop traversal while a **Greycoat Marksman** on the mast's maintenance platform (25 m up) takes shots. His glint telegraphs each one. The player can counter-snipe him, which is hard with irons, or move from water tank to water tank. Two Legion riflemen guard a rooftop machine-gun nest made of sandbags around an empty tripod. Then the **sunset moment**: the player keeps control at forced walk speed for about 25 s, and the Clockmaker's broadcast comes onto the squad net.
- **Squad:** They cross the roofs in single file and call out the sniper. At the last parapet they crouch and look at the compound. If the player looks back west at the sun, the squad looks too.
- **Encounter (3):** 1 Greycoat Marksman and 2 Legion Riflemen. Peak alive 3.
- **Objectives / markers:** `REACH THE STATION WALL` → (after sunset) `BREACH POINT` (marker on the wall, 40 m). The **markers change from amber to pale green** after sunset; the HUD's palette follows the light.
- **Radio:** #30–#36. The Clockmaker broadcast plays, Calder's vision pulses, and glitch insert #36 plays.
- **Transition:** The team drops down a stair into the alley along the station's west wall. Tinker runs to the wall with a satchel charge.

### Beat 6 — THE RELAY YARD (breach and courtyard)
- **Location:** The compound inside a 4 m wall topped with razor wire. The yard holds a generator shed, cable drums, a fuel bowser, sandbagged positions, four **floodlight towers**, and the gatehouse on the south wall. The **mast** rises from a fenced base, its guy wires slicing up through the frame and its red aviation lights blinking.
- **Light:** 18:45, -1°. Afterglow: a pink-orange western sky, a cobalt zenith, no direct sun. The yard is lit by the sky, the burning technical's glow back in the square, and then the floodlights.
- **Gameplay:** Tinker blows the wall (scripted dust cloud, a 0.3 s slow-mo hit, tinnitus). The team pours into the yard. When a Legion soldier shouts "Lamba!", the **floodlights slam on** with heavy bloom and lens flare. Enemies firing from under them get an accuracy bonus. **Shooting out the 4 floods** (each one is a destructible light with sparks and a buzzing die-off) takes away that bonus and gives the yard back to the dark. It's a lighting mechanic that's also a set dressing win.
- **Squad:** They come through the breach in sequence (node A, rubble), then hold the cable drums (node B) and push to the hall door (node C) when the yard is clear.
- **Encounter (8):** 4 Legion Riflemen and 1 Legion Gunner (gatehouse PKM) are present at the breach. 3 Greycoat Advisors come out of the gatehouse after 20 s or once 3 enemies are down. Peak alive 6.
- **Objectives / markers:** `STAND CLEAR` (a safe-zone ring during the charge) → `CLEAR THE YARD` → `ENTER THE RELAY HALL` (door marker).
- **Radio:** #37–#41.
- **Transition:** The squad stacks on the Relay Hall's steel double door.

### Beat 7 — THE RELAY HALL
- **Location:** The ground floor. An Art Deco switch hall with rows of teletypes, telegraph keys under glass, a brass world-time clock wall (every clock stopped, of course), tungsten pendant lamps, and ceiling fans. Harrou's office is on the east end: a desk with a typewriter, filing cabinets, and a map of the border with shipments marked in grease pencil.
- **Light:** Interior tungsten at 2,700 K, warm and dirty. Through the high windows the sky is going blue.
- **Gameplay:** **Slow-mo door breach**: the player kicks the door and gets 1.5 s at 0.35 time scale against 3 enemies, with audio pitched down. CQB through the switch hall follows. The player takes **the Ledgers** (INTEL 1/3, held interact, a polaroid-style intel card appears). Two more enemies come down the stairs.
- **Squad:** They breach with the player and clear the corners. Sparrow reads the ledger aloud and Hollis goes quiet (#43–#44).
- **Encounter (5):** At the breach, 2 Legion and 1 Greycoat. 2 Legion come down the stairs. Peak alive 3.
- **Objectives / markers:** `BREACH` → `RECOVER THE LEDGERS` (intel icon) → `SEARCH UPSTAIRS`.
- **Radio:** #42–#44.
- **Transition:** A spiral staircase with a cage lift shaft leads to the upper floor.

### Beat 8 — THE REEL (upstairs screening room)
- **Location:** The station staff's old screening room: 20 velvet seats, a pull-down screen, a 16mm projector on a table, and a wall rack of film cans all labelled **S-3**.
- **Light:** Darkness, a desk lamp, then the projector beam (a cone mesh with additive dust) once it's running.
- **Gameplay:** No combat. The player threads a reel when Sparrow asks, which triggers cinematic MID-B, "The Reel" (§6.4). Afterward the player takes the cans (INTEL 2/3).
- **Squad:** Sparrow photographs the screen. Tinker watches, quiet for once. Hollis guards the stairs with his back to the screen, so he's the only one who doesn't see it.
- **Objectives / markers:** `RECOVER THE FILM` → `GET TO THE BASEMENT`.
- **Radio:** #45–#47.
- **Transition:** A service stair goes down past the ground floor into the basement. The ticking gets louder.

### Beat 9 — THE CLOCKMAKER (basement transmitter and vault)
- **Location:** A 25 x 20 m concrete basement. The **transmitter** is a wall of vacuum-tube racks glowing orange behind mesh, an **ON AIR** lamp, and a reel-to-reel tape loop running the interval signal: a ticking clock and the music-box chime. Above everything is a speaker relaying the broadcast into the room. There's a 1950s vault safe set into the wall. Two stairwells (east and west) and a cable-shaft hatch lead in.
- **Light:** Orange glow from the tubes, red pulses from the ON AIR lamp, flashlight beams. It's the most "night interior" space so far.
- **Gameplay:** **Defend Sparrow for 90 s** while she drills the safe (a progress ring shows on the drill marker). Waves come down the stairwells and the hatch. Once the drill finishes she pulls the **Codebook** (INTEL 3/3). Then Hollis orders the player to **destroy the transmitter** by shooting the glowing tube racks, which shatter in sprays of sparks. The broadcast cuts out mid-number (#53). The room goes dark except for emergency lights.
- **Squad:** Sparrow is at the safe and unavailable. Hollis covers the west stairs, Tinker the east. The player floats between them. Tinker shouts which side is under pressure.
- **Encounter (9):** Wave 1 is 3 Legion down the west stairs. Wave 2 is 3 Greycoats down the east stairs, with a grenade. Wave 3 is 2 Legion from the hatch and 1 Greycoat from the east. Peak alive 5.

## 7. Time of day, lighting and weather plan

### 7.1 Schedule (tied to mission progress, not the wall clock)
The sun's position is driven by **beat index plus progress within the beat**, so a player who lingers can't freeze golden hour, and a speedrunner still sees the sunset on the roofs. The sun drops about 0.2° per story-minute, which is realistic for about 30°N in October.

| Stage | Beats | Story clock | Sun elev. | Key light | Sky (HDRI) | Fog / haze | Exposure (Post Exposure) | Practical lights |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Golden** | 1–3 | 17:58–18:20 | 8° → 4.5° | Directional at 258° az, 3,400 K → 2,800 K, URP intensity 2.0 → 1.6, soft shadows | Golden-hour HDRI, rotated so its sun matches 258° | Warm dust haze, low density, a height falloff toward the salt pan | 0 EV | None. Dust motes and fake light shafts |
| **Sunset** | 4–5 | 18:20–18:40 | 3° → 0° | 2,400 K → 1,900 K, intensity 1.4 → 0 over the last 20 s | Sunset HDRI (high cirrus lit pink and orange). The swap happens inside the cinema | Orange → violet | +0.4 EV | Sodium lamps strike at 18:40. Mast lights. The burning technical |
| **Afterglow** | 6–8 | 18:45–18:54 | -1° → -3° | **No sun.** A shadowless "sky key" directional from 258° at 9,000 K, intensity 0.15 | Dusk HDRI (orange band on the horizon, cobalt overhead) | Violet, denser | +1.0 EV | Floodlights (destructible), tungsten interiors, muzzle-flash lights |
| **Blue hour** | 9–11 | 18:58–19:10 | -4° → -6° | Sky key at 0.08, 10,000 K | Dusk HDRI tinted darker and bluer, or a night HDRI | Deep blue | +1.5 EV plus a cool, slightly desaturated grade (Purkinje shift) | Sodium streets, fires, headlights, tracers, rotor lights |

### 7.2 Techniques (URP 17 on WebGL2)
- **One shadowed directional light.** 2 cascades, about 80 m shadow distance in golden hour, cut to 50 m after sunset when shadows are soft anyway. Medium soft shadows, a 2,048 px map. No shadows from additional lights except, if budget allows, one floodlight in Beat 6.
- **HDRI crossfades with no visible seams.** A small custom skybox shader blends between two cubemaps with a `_Blend` value plus a tint and exposure. **Swaps happen while the player is indoors** (cinema = golden → sunset, Relay Hall = afterglow → blue), so the sun disc in the HDRI never visibly jumps. On the roofs the sunset is "sold" by the **western ridge silhouette** (a backdrop mesh) occluding the sun disc while the light fades. Each HDRI is rotated so its sun lines up with 258°. The lower hemisphere is masked out because terrain covers it.
- **Ambient:** the ambient spherical harmonics are lerped from code between pre-captured probes for each stage (via `RenderSettings.ambientProbe`). **Reflection probes are pre-baked once per stage** (box-projected in interiors) and swapped at the same hidden moments.
- **No auto exposure in URP.** Exposure is authored by hand, per stage and per interior, through local post-processing volumes with blend distances (doorways ease the exposure in and out).
- **Post stack:** ACES tonemapping. Bloom (threshold about 1.0) that makes the sodium lamps, floodlights and sun bleed. **Film grain** (period texture that also hides banding and low-res textures). A light vignette. White-balance keyframes per stage. Subtle chromatic aberration. Depth of field only in cinematics. No motion blur (it costs too much in WebGL).
- **Fake volumetrics:** additive light-shaft card meshes, fading by view angle, in the grove, the market awning gaps, the cinema auditorium and the projector beam. Soft dust particles. Large haze-sheet particles over the salt pan. A cheap height-fog full-screen pass (one depth-based pass) in place of real volumetric fog.
- **Lights at night:** forward rendering with a per-object light limit. Test Forward+ on WebGL2 and fall back to Forward if it fails. **At most 8 dynamic point or spot lights on screen**: 4 pooled muzzle flashes, floodlights, and the nearest sodium lamps. Distant lamps are emissive sprites with bloom and no real light.
- **Ambient occlusion:** baked into textures and props, plus blob and contact-shadow decals under stalls and crates. Optional half-res, low-quality URP SSAO only if it fits the frame budget.
- **Decals** (URP Decal feature in Screen Space mode): grime, water stains, posters, bullet holes, tire marks.

### 7.3 Weather
Clear and dry, with a light 3–5 m/s west wind, so laundry, awnings and dust all drift the same way. The HDRIs carry **high cirrus** to catch sunset color (the sky goes pink and orange without any cloud simulation). There are small dust devils on the salt pan (particles) and, optionally, heat shimmer over the salt pan in Beat 1 only. No rain, sandstorm or volumetric clouds: they're expensive, and clear air is what makes golden hour read as golden hour.

### 7.4 Why this showcases realism within WebGL limits
1. **Low sun hides cheap geometry.** Long raking shadows and strong warm/cool contrast give modular boxes and parapets form and depth. A flat noon would show every simple mesh.
2. **Golden hour fakes global illumination.** Warm key plus cool sky fill is the look people read as "photoreal," and it comes almost entirely from one directional light plus an HDRI ambient. No baked or realtime GI is needed, which matters because dynamic time of day rules out baked direct lighting and WebGL has no compute shaders for adaptive probe volumes.
3. **Haze limits draw distance.** Dust haze in the golden half and darkness in the second half both shorten the visible range, so there are fewer draw calls and LODs get away with less.
4. **Night is cheap and dramatic.** After sunset, shadow cascades drop, bloom carries the emotion, and a handful of practical lights (sodium, floods, muzzle flashes) give the mission its best-looking frames at the lowest cost.
5. **Transitions are hidden in interiors.** Every risky lighting swap happens where the player can't see the sky.
6. **The light is a story beat.** The sunset is *the* mid-mission cinematic, and it costs a light lerp, an HDRI blend and a lamp-strike script, not a destruction set piece.

### 7.5 Audio realism (WebGL-aware)
- **Unity's Web platform plays basic AudioSources** (volume, pitch, 3D panning) but **does not run Audio Mixer DSP, filters or reverb zones** (per Unity's Web audio docs; verify on 6000.6). So **everything is baked offline** with a Python and numpy/scipy pipeline (no ffmpeg needed):
  - **Two variants of every squad line:** dry, and radio-filtered (300–3,400 Hz band-pass, soft clipping, squelch head and tail). The VO manager crossfades two AudioSources by distance: dry within 8 m, radio beyond 15 m. Squadmates sound like they're next to you when they are and on the net when they aren't.
  - **Gunshot layers:** close, mid and far, each with three baked tail variants (narrow-street slapback, open square, interior room), picked by trigger volumes.
  - **Harrou's PA:** baked with a horn-speaker EQ and a long multi-tap echo.
  - **The Clockmaker:** AM-filtered with wow-and-flutter for radio playback, plus one clean version for the climax.
- **Ambient beds change with the light:** golden hour has wind, cicadas, a distant dog and the curfew siren's tail. At sunset the cicadas stop, crickets start, and sodium lamps buzz. Then generator hum in the compound, and at night fire crackle, distant shouting and the rotor.
- **The motif:** the three-note music-box chime is the Clockmaker's interval signal, the score's main theme, and the outro's last sound.
- At most about 24 audio voices play at once. Distant shots are culled by priority.

## 11. Scope and risk notes

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Build files over 15 MB** (Unity's `.data` and `.wasm` are single large files) | Hosting rejects the publish | Split them into ≤ 14 MB parts. A custom `index.html` fetches the parts, concatenates them into a Blob and passes an object URL as `dataUrl` to the loader. Or load Addressables bundles of ≤ 14 MB each. Use Brotli or Gzip compression with **Decompression Fallback** on. Budget the whole build at ≤ 180 MB |
| **No Audio Mixer DSP in WebGL** | Radio filtering, reverb and occlusion won't work at runtime | Bake every variant offline (§7.5). Do ducking with volume curves in the VO manager |
| **HDRI sun versus directional-light mismatch** | Visible "two suns" or a skybox jump | Rotate HDRIs to 258°, swap only indoors, and occlude the sun disc with the ridge silhouette during the live sunset |
| **No realtime or adaptive-probe GI in WebGL** | Interiors look flat | Box-projected reflection probes per stage, hand-placed fill lights in interiors, baked AO in materials, contact decals |
| **Point-light count at night** | Lights pop or the frame rate tanks | At most 8 dynamic lights on screen. Distant lamps are emissive plus bloom. Pooled muzzle lights. Test Forward+ and fall back to Forward |
| **Alpha overdraw in the grove and laundry** | Fill-rate spikes on laptops | About 40 trees with aggressive LODs. Alpha-clip rather than blend. Laundry as cloth-card quads |
| **AI on rooftops** | Navmesh pathing bugs | Enemies on roofs use **static perch nodes only**. The squad moves on roofs by node chain with navmesh links over gaps |
| **Squad pathing in the narrow market** | Allies block the player | Arcade-side node lanes, a no-stop zone in the middle of the street, push-through collision, and teleport catch-up |
| **fal 3D quality varies** | Bad topology or textures | Generate 2–3 candidates per prop and keep the best. Hero props (projector, safe, transmitter, helicopter) fall back to [P] primitives with CC0 materials. Poly budgets: props 2–8k triangles, hero props 15–30k, weapons 10–20k |
| **Invented language spoken by TTS** | Odd pronunciation | That's fine; barks should be unintelligible. Subtitles with translations in brackets carry the meaning |
| **Slow-mo and audio** | Pitch artifacts | Pitch-shift only the breach mix and keep VO at normal speed |
| **Browser input** | Pointer lock and audio blocked | The diegetic click gate (§6.0). Re-lock on click after Esc. Test Safari WebGL2 |
| **Sensitivity** | Unintended real-world readings | The §1 guardrails, plus a final art pass that checks every text texture is in the invented script or Latin |
| **Performance targets** | | 60 fps at 1080p on an M1/M2 MacBook in Chrome (dynamic render scale down to 0.75). At most about 600 SRP batches, at most about 1.2M visible triangles, 7 enemies alive by design, about 1 GB WebGL heap |

**Cut list if behind (in order):** (1) M60D door-gun gameplay; Beat 11 becomes a pure cinematic. (2) Slow-mo breach. (3) Rooftop marksman; Beat 5 becomes traversal plus the sunset only. (4) Destructible floodlights; the floods stay on as static set dressing. (5) The live projector film becomes still frames on a CRT. (6) Grove trees reduce to the pump house plus irrigation walls. **Never cut:** the sunset moment, the radio numbers, the glitch inserts, the intro and outro chair scenes, the debrief.
